# CypherClash Round 3: Case HELIX Gameplay Guide

> **Spoilers ahead.** The first sections explain the rules and recommended play order. The complete solution is in the final section.

This guide describes the active Round 3 investigation in the web app. The case has ten suspects, 28 available evidence records (EV01-EV28), two required case questions and four optional extras, a shared team file, and a 45-minute timer. The intended main route should take a focused pair about 35–40 minutes, leaving a few minutes for the final partner approval. All 28 clues remain in the game; the shorter route does not require finding every clue or interviewing every suspect.

## 1. The Objective

A restricted set of HELIX research files was moved to a temporary holding area inside Veyra Research Complex at 2:13 a.m. The transfer receipt lists no approved project number. Two minutes later, the short summary no longer showed that temporary maintenance access had been used.

Work with your partner to determine:

- Who arranged the file transfer.
- How the computer system allowed it.
- Why the later summary was changed.
- Which other explanations the records rule out.

The receipt places the files in an internal holding area. It does **not** prove that they were sent outside the complex.

## 2. Start and Team Roles

The Detective joins first and receives an invitation code that expires after 10 minutes. Give that code to the second player, who joins as Analyst from their own browser session. If it expires before the Analyst joins, the Detective can create a new code. Both players work on the same saved case, but each has different duties and evidence access.

| Role         | Main duties                                                                                               | Main case areas                                                                  |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Detective    | Choose one person to interview and inspect physical and written records. Further interviews are optional. | Physical Records, Documents, Interviews, and the optional badge-record question. |
| Analyst      | Review computer records, clocks, access history, and file-transfer records.                               | Computer Records and the clock, identity, and summary questions.                 |
| Both players | Compare findings, put events in order, assess explanations, write the report, and review it together.     | Case Board, Timeline, Compare Explanations, Final Case Report.                   |

Discoveries, completed questions, interview answers, and report activity are saved to the shared case. The other player can see the shared progress. Each role can be claimed only once for the active case attempt. The Detective invitation code expires after 10 minutes and can be renewed if needed. The team must have completed the earlier rounds before joining Round 3.

The round lasts 45 minutes. After it ends, the case can still be reviewed, but players cannot make changes.

## 3. Recommended Order of Play

1. Join as Detective, then invite your partner to join as Analyst.
2. Find the report’s eight required evidence records and their prerequisites. Also collect records that directly address the two alternative explanations you plan to reject.
3. On **Computer Records**, solve the two required Analyst questions: **Correct the Clock and Order the Events** and **What Did the Summary Leave Out?** The identity, badge-record, separate event-order, and best-explanation questions are optional extras.
4. The Detective interviews any one person of their choice and records one follow-up answer. Further interviews are optional; there is no requirement to question all ten suspects.
5. Optionally assess explanations using any 2–8 different records your team has found. This separate workflow is not required to submit the final report.
6. Complete the final report: identify the person, explain the access and motive, enter the event order, cite the required records, and reject two alternatives with relevant records and a written explanation for each.
7. One player submits the report. The other player must review and approve it from their own session. The submitting player cannot approve their own report a second time.

The Case Board shows progress for records, the two required questions, and optional leads. A follow-up answer may also announce that it unlocked a new record. Open the record from its evidence card to read its full description.

## 5. Suspect Interview Guide

The Detective chooses whom to interview. Ask at least one available follow-up before submitting the final report; that single answer can be about any suspect. All further interviews are optional. A topic marked “Show” requires that record to be in the shared case first. Each topic can be recorded once. The answer is saved and shown to both players; some answers also unlock evidence automatically.

## 6. Required and Optional Case Questions

There are six questions in the interface, but only two are required for the report. The other four are optional extras. Complete a question only after your role has found its prerequisite records. The exact answer values below are accepted by the game; the screen uses readable labels for them.

### 6.1 Required: Correct the Clock and Order the Events

- **Required records:** EV05 and EV06. Analyst role.
- **Clock answer:** 2 minutes fast.
- **Order:**
  1. Maintenance prepared (`maintenance-prepared`).
  2. Badge recorded (`credential-event`).
  3. Computers exchanged a message (`service-relay`).
  4. Temporary access became active (`exception-active`).
  5. Access was accepted and files moved (`token-accepted-export`).
  6. Summary was changed (`audit-view-changed`).

#### Next actions after the clock question

The clock card is on the Analyst’s **Computer Records** page. After **Check my answer** accepts it, follow this order. Evidence IDs refer to the labels on the cards. For an available card, use **Read this record and add it to the shared case**.

1. **Detective: collect EV09 and EV14.** Open **Documents** and add EV09, **Leena’s Note Was Written Before It Reached the Archive**, and EV14, **Gabriel’s Approved Repair Job Ended at 9:14 p.m.** Neither has an evidence prerequisite.
2. **Detective: complete one interview.** Open **Interviews**. Under **Who would you like to speak with?**, choose Mara Chen. This records the required interview and adds EV01 to the shared case. No second interview is needed.
3. **Analyst: collect EV07 and EV08.** On **Computer Records**, EV06 should already be in the shared case from the clock question. Add EV07, **The Access System Accepted a HELIX Digital Pass**, then add EV08, **The Later Summary Leaves Out Maintenance Access**. EV08 requires EV07.
4. **Analyst: finish the second required question.** On **Computer Records**, open **What Did the Summary Leave Out?** It becomes available when EV08 and EV09 are in the shared case. Select **Temporary maintenance access**; **The original access record**; **10:06 p.m.** for when Leena wrote the note; **8:40 a.m.** for when the archive received it; and **2:15 a.m.** for when the summary changed. Click **Check my answer**.
5. **Collect the remaining required report records.** With EV14 found, the Analyst adds EV17. 7. **Submit the report.** Open **Final Case Report**. Fill in the required values in Section 8 and click **Send your report for partner review**. Your partner then clicks **Review and approve this report** from their own session.

## 7. Saving an Assessment

An assessment is your team’s current opinion about one explanation, not a final verdict. For each explanation:

1. Choose a status: records support it, records conflict with it, not enough information, it does not explain the transfer, or the record may be incomplete/unreliable.
2. Select **any 2-8 distinct records that your team has already found**. You are not restricted to a theory-specific checklist.
3. Select **Save my assessment**. You may save an incorrect or uncertain assessment and later replace it with a different status or different records.

The server rejects records the team has not found, duplicate record IDs, fewer than two records, or more than eight. A mistaken assessment by itself does not count as a wrong puzzle answer or reduce the score. Assessments are optional and do not grant report credit by themselves; the final report must independently cite and explain its rejected alternatives.

### Required report citations

The final report must include these eight IDs: **EV01, EV07, EV14, EV17, EV24, EV26, EV27, EV28**. Together they cover four evidence groups: physical, digital, documents, and connections between records. The report form allows additional records your team has found.

### Rejecting alternatives

The report must reject at least two alternatives. For each one, include at least two relevant discovered records among the report citations and name those record IDs in a separate written explanation of at least 40 characters. The report checks the selected theory, record relevance, and presence of that explanation. A separate hypothesis assessment is not required and does not substitute for report citations or reasoning. A straightforward pair is:

- **Sofia’s database search:** cite EV03 and EV11. Her search broke a rule, but it returned system information rather than research files.
- **Gabriel’s repair work:** cite at least two of EV14, EV18, and EV22. His approved job ended at 9:14 p.m.; the later access request was separate.

Other alternatives have their own relevant records: Mara (EV01, EV10, EV23), Victor (EV02, EV04), and a computer fault (EV08, EV19, EV24). The server checks that the selected theory has at least two relevant cited records and report-specific written reasoning.

After submission, the other player must review and approve the report from their own account. The submitting player cannot approve their own report a second time. When both roles approve, the case closes and the score is submitted.

## 9. Scoring and Saving

- The round timer remains 45 minutes; the shorter required route is designed to take about 35–40 minutes, leaving time for discussion and partner approval.
- The report starts at 100 points.
- Each counted wrong attempt reduces the final report score by 5 points, down to a minimum of 0. Incorrect answers to optional or required puzzles and report submissions rejected by the case evidence checks count as wrong attempts.
- Saving a hypothesis assessment with a mistaken status or choice of found records is allowed and does not itself incur that penalty.
- Case progress is stored on the server and shared between both roles. The case becomes read-only when the round is paused, closed, or past its deadline.

## 10. Complete Solution (Spoilers)

**Idris Khan arranged the unauthorized transfer and changed the later summary to conceal it.** He prepared a temporary maintenance-access exception, and the system records connect that request to the access used in the file movement. Idris is named as the staff reviewer on the signed request (EV28). At 2:13 a.m., the system accepted the HELIX digital pass and the restricted files were moved into an internal holding area without an approved project number (EV07 and EV27). At 2:15 a.m., the summary was changed so it no longer showed the maintenance-access detail present in the original record (EV08 and EV24). Idris knew a rules review was scheduled for 3:00 a.m. (EV26), giving him a reason to hide the connection before it was reviewed.

The misleading leads each have a separate explanation:

- **Mara:** H-204 appears in the badge records, but a badge entry does not identify its holder. Mara says she left the badge at a shared sign-out desk after a private meeting. Her private omission explains why she was evasive; it does not prove she carried out the transfer.
- **Sofia:** She searched the database after hours without following the proper process. Her search returned system information, not HELIX files, and did not start the transfer.
- **Victor:** The missing patrol check happened during a recorder reset. Correcting the camera time places the gap away from the file transfer.
- **Anika:** She used a nearby corridor at 12:42 a.m. to retrieve a notebook, well before the access and transfer. She hid the rule-breaking route, not the file move.
- **Gabriel:** His supervised relay repair ended at 9:14 p.m. The later temporary-access request was separate from his signed repair work.
- **Tomas:** His partial message looked suspicious, but the full conversation names the university’s approved storage site and says the request would be checked against the rules. It is unrelated to the 2:13 a.m. transfer.
- **Nisha:** She delayed sharing a separate sample-quality note to protect the program’s reputation. She could approve research projects but could not grant temporary computer access.
- **Leena:** Her note was written at 10:06 p.m. and filed by the archive at 8:40 a.m. The filing time does not mean she wrote it after the incident, and she could not change the original access record.
- **Evan:** The shared computer was unassigned during part of the shift handoff. The log does not identify who used it, so the workstation name alone does not implicate him.
